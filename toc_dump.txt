Child[44] (style=, bms=[]): Mobile Robot Positioning: Functional Development and Experimental Validation of an ESP32-S3 Mobile Robot with MPU6050 Odometry, Encoders, and Graphical User Interface.
Child[45] (style=, bms=[]): This professional residency project presents the embedded design, instrumentation, and experimental validation of a low-cost 4WD mobile robot prototype developed under academic and laboratory resource constraints. Centered on an ESP32-S3 microcontroller, dual slotted optical encoders (LM393 with hardware pulse counting via PCNT), and an MPU6050 6-DOF inertial measurement unit, the platform implements an asymmetric dual-core architecture: a deterministic 100 Hz synchronous super-cycle in FreeRTOS on Core 1 dedicated to sensor filtering, heading PID control, and dynamic pivot maneuvers, decoupled from asynchronous WebSocket communications and JSON telemetry (protocol robot-s3-steps-v3) managed on Core 0. Forensics state vectors are persisted in real time into an SQLite database operating under Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) and audited post-run via ADB synchronization. Physical track testing across 94 discrete route steps confirmed effective heading stabilization, achieving a mean angular error of -0.04° ± 0.89° (with 93.5% of endpoints contained within a ±1.5° tolerance). Rather than claiming ideal autonomy, systematic metric evaluation revealed clear hardware boundaries: a step-command execution success rate of 79.7% (94 of 118 route segments completed without triggering stall watchdogs; 83.2% global protocol success across 268 total system commands) and a systematic longitudinal slip error of -8.63 ± 8.53 cm attributable to uncalibrated Instantaneous Center of Rotation (ICR) dynamics and tire viscoelastic compliance on smooth tile surfaces. The primary contribution of this work lies in delivering an open, fully instrumented, and reproducible cyber-physical baseline (backed by 13,930 forensic database records, 74/74 unit tests, and a live web technical atlas) that quantitatively isolates control strengths from mechanical friction limits, establishing an empirical foundation for future state-estimation research.
